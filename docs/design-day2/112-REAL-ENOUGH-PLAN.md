> **Revised 2026-10-02, the same day, on the first review.** Three things changed. **Keys are in scope:** the hosted guards, evaluators and readers that have run as stand-ins since WP42 are lit with real credentials (Phase AS), so the "dark" half of the catalogue is measured, not only connectable. **The test data gets a phase of its own** (Phase AU): the population is wide but the desks' cases, personas, scripts and scenarios are few and hand-written, and a live model over a hundred fixed cases is a live model over a hundred fixed cases. **The record comes first** (Phase AR): before any live run is recorded, every sensor the run can carry is in place, counted, and tested for coverage, and a run can be read as a story by someone who was not there. ## 0. The ask, and the answer in one paragraph

The ask was three things: judge how well the synthetic bank simulates a UK retail bank's typical workflows for the purpose of evaluating AI automation, safety and guardrails; name what is under-developed or worth another sprint; and set out the tests that would then produce design recommendations for securing real-world agentic bank workflows. The judgment: **the bank is wide and honest, and its actors are not real enough for its numbers to carry outside it.** Eight journeys over a cited population, 69 catalogued techniques with 61 shipped, 84 control-map rows, sixteen reference experiments, a benchmark over 1,408 adversarial rows and a Gate that can govern a foreign agent — and every effect in `docs/evidence/` was produced by a scripted bot, a planted error rate and a person who always approves, over a few dozen hand-written cases per desk, with every hosted guard a stand-in. No live model has run at scale; no journey moves money; no desk holds a second customer the assistant could reach by mistake; no journey has two agents in it. These are the four shapes a real agentic bank workflow has and this one does not, and the register's ±100-point effects say so. The sprint, in order: **the record made complete** (every sensor counted and covered, every run readable as a story), **the lights on** (real keys for the hosted services, the benchmark and the checkpoints recorded), **live actors** (the 122B on the DGX Spark as the tier, recorded to cassettes CI replays; a person who sometimes says no; a bot that errs the way models do), **the data made wide** (cases drawn, not written; corpora grown and blind-labelled; scenarios and scripts as templates over a seed), **the payments desk** (the first journey that moves money, with the scam conversation in front of it), **the bank among agents** (a second customer in the room, a second agent in the journey, delegated authority on the trace), **the controls that catch decisions**, and **the exit** (the Gate over a live agent, the recommendation as a file). Then the fourteen tests in §9 run on it.

### 2.3 The world

- **One customer per desk.** `BankExtra.bank` is one `BankCase`; `fs-bank/crm`'s `read-customer` takes no customer id; `read-record` takes a record id on *the* customer. The graph line reaches a case's parties, never a second account-holder. **The assistant cannot reach the wrong customer because there is no wrong customer to reach.** Cross-customer disclosure — the first thing a bank's security team asks of an agent over a CRM — is structurally untestable today.
- **Truth is honest.** `checkDesk`'s truth independence (WP111), `run.finished.truth` the only event carrying it, every desk audited (`102-…` §5). This is the bank's strongest property and the plan leaves it alone.
- **The population is calibrated and cited** (`CALIBRATION`, 20,000 customers in test, `BOOK_INCIDENCES`), with a clock, books per desk and a seven-desk bank day in CI. Nothing here needs a sprint; it needs its reader.
- **Nothing real** holds (hard rule 9; `checkSynthetic` over every file, `synthetic.credential` since WP151).

### 2.4 The test data

The population is wide; what the desks put in front of the assistant is narrow.

| Layer | What there is | How wide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Customers** | `bankCase(seed)` from the calibration table: 20,000 drawn in test, each with accounts, a transaction stream, a bureau file, hidden and revealed records | **Wide.** The one layer that is generated, not written |
| **Cases a desk shows** | A fixed set of *case kinds* per desk in `world/cases.ts` (`ADVICE_CASE_KINDS` 12, fraud 11, lending 11, disputes 8, collections 7, onboarding 5, servicing 5), each a hand-written shape that `assemble<Desk>Case(kind, …)` lays over a customer drawn from the seed through `bankCase`; a book draws its items the same way | **Half wide.** The customer varies with the seed; the complication does not. A book of 886 complaints is 886 customers over a dozen kinds, one complication each, never two at once |
| **Personas** | Ten in `fs-bank/personas.ts`, each with fixed lines keyed by cue (`what-is-risk`, `press`, `threat`, `urgency`, …); the desks' own persona sets on top | **Fixed.** Ten voices, each saying the same thing to every assistant |
| **Scripts** | `CounterpartScript`: on each cue (`agent-says-matches`, `agent-asks`, `action-performed`, `tick-at-least`, `always`) the first rule whose `when` matches fires, one fixed line; `adversarialScript` speaks a corpus's attack rows. A script *can* stage its lines over a call with `tick-at-least` | **Linear.** No script branches on what the assistant did two turns ago; no persona changes its mind; there is no second voice; and no shipped script plays a groom over several turns, though the shape allows it |
| **Scenarios** | Hand-written, each carrying `injections[]` (so several per scenario), with a tick only on `heard` and `provider-fault`: advice 30, fraud 17, lending 16, the five Phase AO cards, the four injection scenarios, a few per Phase AA desk | **Hand-written.** No scenario is a template over a seed, so a scenario campaign is the same N scenarios every run |
| **Corpora** | The servicing three (95–306 rows), six desk corpora of 100 rows, seven adversarial corpora (1,408 rows), each blind-labelled twice | **Small, well-labelled.** A hundred rows per desk is a test, not a distribution; κ ≥ 0.98 says the rows are clear to models, not that they are varied |

A live model over this data will tell us about this data. Phase AU widens the three narrow layers without touching the one that is wide.

### 2.5 The sensors and the record

What a run already carries (`core/src/schemas/events.ts`, 32 event types): `prompt.composed` with every message; `think.started` with the wire model, provider and cartridge; `think.completed` with the response — text, tool call, `raw`, `finishReason`, `logprobs`; `decision` with the thought and the call; `decision.fault` when planted; `tool.executed` with arguments, result, data and `durationMs`; `action.performed` with its attestation and redaction; `guardrail.checked` / `tripped` / `external` with component, point and cause; `content.marked`; `memory.updated` with its source; `approval.*` and `elevation.*` with who and why; `stage.started` / `completed` / `overdue`; `reader.answered` with confidence; `disclosure.given`; `world.changed`; `run.started` with principal, gate, build digest and goal dial; `run.finished` with truth. Every event is on the bus, in the bundle under a digest, in the OTel mapping, and readable in the Run Lab with Explain and *Fork from this tick*. This is a strong record, and it was built for a scripted bot.

What a live run needs that the record does not carry:

- **The think call's own measures.** `think.completed` carries the response and nothing else — no `durationMs` (the tool call has one); `think.started` carries the wire model, provider and cartridge but no `temperature`, `maxTokens` or seed. A cassette *hit* leaves no mark on the trace (a miss does), so a replayed run and a live one are indistinguishable there — by design, since a replay must reproduce the recording's digest (`103-…` §3) — and today only the campaign's cell says which a reader is reading.
- **The seat's own line.** In a duo the counterpart is a seat with a trace of its own, and its lines are its `action.performed`. Inside a desk — every scenario run without a duo, and every book — `advanceCounterpart` runs in the desk's `perform`, and what the visitor said is desk state (`world.changed`): no event carries the persona, the cue that fired and the rule that chose it.
- **The reviewer's draw.** `ReviewerAnswer` carries the person's accuracy, automation bias and reason; the draw that produced an answer (which rate row, which roll) is not on the trace, so a wrong human answer and a planted fault are told apart only by `decision.fault`'s presence.
- **The stage's values over the cap.** A stage's input or output over `VALUE_CAP` (16 KiB) is kept as its digest alone (`workflow/src/run.ts`), documented and deliberate; a reader of the record gets a hash where a prompt or a long result was, and nothing retains the value beside the run.
- **The story.** Explain is per tick; the assurance pack is per bank; the campaign report is per gate. Nothing renders one run, or one case through a journey with its handoffs, as a narrative a person reads top to bottom: what arrived, what the assistant was told, what it thought, what it asked, what it did, what stopped it, who approved, what the truth was. `craftabot report` renders the safety case and incidents, not a case.
- **The sensor inventory.** There is a Control Inventory with an orphan rule; there is no Sensor Inventory. Nothing tests that every event type a run can carry is observed at least once over the bank day, so a sensor could go quiet and nothing would say.

### 2.6 The controls and their measurement

- **Built:** 69 catalogue entries (61 shipped, 1 connectable, 1 blueprint, 6 not applicable), 84 control-map rows, 44 policy cards on the desks, ~50 evaluators, 14 component adapters, eight guard services on the shell, the Gate with five presets, eleven rule readers, hosted and LLM readers, 25 ceilings, `autonomy.enforce`.
- **Measured:** sixteen designs, every effect reproduced at full size on 2026-10-02. **Verdicts: 0 supported, 9 inconclusive, 7 not-supported.** Not one control reads *supported* on this bank. The reasons are the findings of the last two plans, and they are all about the actors: the scripted bot errs nowhere a card looks; the planted error has no cohort or context to be caught by; the desks' cards check sequence and approvals, not decisions (`111-…` Phase AP's exit); the person always approves.
- **The hosted guards** are *connectable* and *unmeasured* until a cassette is recorded with a key; Llama Guard 3 is measured locally (precision 92%, recall 17%) and the keyword baseline at recall 26%. The benchmark's instrument is sound and its subjects are mostly stand-ins.
- **Fairness, drift, calibration, human load** are in `@craftabot/metrics` with a validation suite. They are correct and, on this bank, measure nothing that moves.

### 2.7 The exit to the real world

`@craftabot/governance` 1.0.0 installs from a tarball into `examples/plain-node-agent`; the Gate governs `examples/gated-agent` to four outcomes. Both agents are scripted. **No live agent has ever been gated**, and no result in the repository says what the Gate costs in latency or tokens on a wire that carries a model's own tool calls. Purpose 2 (`00-…`: components *exported for real-world use*) has a door and nobody has walked through it.

## 4. The target

1. **Every run is readable and complete.** Every sensor the engine has is inventoried and seen firing over the bank day; a live think call carries its parameters, its duration and whether it was replayed; the counterpart, the reviewer and the error model each say what they did and why on the trace; any run or case renders as a story with the truth at the end.
2. **Every hosted service is lit, measured and recorded**, with its cassette replaying in CI and its live checkpoint recorded with a date, so *connectable* becomes *measured* on the catalogue, the Rack and the benchmark page.
3. **Every reference design has a live level** — the 122B on the DGX Spark — recorded to a provider cassette CI replays with no key, so the register's columns read *scripted · fallible · live* and the live column is the one a reader believes; a frontier brain sits beside it on three designs as the comparison.
4. **The data is as wide as the population.** Cases drawn through generated layouts, personas drawn from cohorts with varied words, scripts that branch, scenarios as templates over a seed, corpora of a thousand rows per desk blind-labelled by a second model and sampled by a person.
5. **The bank moves money.** A payments desk with the scam conversation in front of the payment, the hold the fraud desk inherits, and the first `irreversible` operation a journey calls under a ceiling.
6. **The assistant can be wrong about the customer.** A second customer in the room, a delegated agent in the journey, and the controls that keep each to its own: measured, with their price.
7. **The controls catch decisions**, so a stack that does nothing reads *inconclusive* for the right reason, and at least one control on this bank reads *supported*.
8. **The fourteen tests in §9 can run** on the sprint's output, each as a file, and each producing a recommendation with the evidence under it.

## 6. Decisions (defaults stated; the build starts on them unless the review changes one)

- **D1 — Which model the live tier is. Confirmed on review: the DGX Spark's 122B (`dgx-spark/giant-qwen`, `Qwen3.5-122B-A10B-NVFP4`; `99-…` §5) through `@craftabot/pack-dgx-spark`,** with the 35B (`dgx-spark/quick-qwen`, `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-NVFP4`) as the counterpart's and the data generator's model and as a second brain level on `lending-stack`. The pack routes by model directory and fails over between the two units, so a recording survives a unit being switched between modes (`99-…` §3). Keyless, local, already in the repository behind `--config`, spends nothing, and closer to what a bank would run inside its own boundary than a frontier API is. The cassette's `note` names the model; nothing claims a frontier model's behaviour from a local one.
- **D2 — The shaped error rates. Default: assumptions, stated as such,** three rows per desk, each `review: 'pending'`, and each replaced by the live tier's measured shape as WP168 records it. The shaped model is a stand-in for the live tier where a design cannot afford the live tier's size.
- **D3 — The second customer. Default: `neighbours` drawn from the population, never from another case's truth,** two per desk, with their own records and classification. The alternative — every desk over the whole population — is the Monitor's shape, not a case's, and would make every prompt carry a bank.
- **D4 — Delegation's mechanism. Default: one `core` change, the `delegate` executor over the existing session group,** with Radio as the channel and `peer-auth` as the check, not a new message bus. Hard rule 4: packs contribute content, and a second agent is a mechanism.
- **D5 — The payments desk's scam label. Default: the PSR's APP categories in truth, hidden, with the counterpart's script the only way they reach the prompt.** The desk never knows a payment is a scam; the questions it asks and the warnings it gives are measured against the label, as the fraud desk's alert decision is.
- **D6 — Where a test's recommendation lives. Default: a file under `tests/` beside `experiments/`, read by `craftabot recommend`,** so a recommendation is reproducible from its designs as an effect is from its runs. A recommendation is never typed into a page.
- **D7 — The Workshop and the live tier. Default: the Workshop replays cassettes and never records,** keeping hard rule 2's shape — no key reaches the browser for a campaign — and the harness the only recorder.
- **D8 — The frontier model's place. Default: a comparison brain on three designs, never the tier.** It needs a key and money and tells us about one vendor's model; the question the bank asks is about a model a bank could run. It is recorded once, beside the 122B, so the two columns can be read together.
- **D9 — Where the keys live. Default: `.env` on this machine, read by the harness as `CRAFTABOT_CREDENTIAL_<ID>`, never in the repository, a cassette, a story or a span,** with the key-leak test the proof over every artefact the sprint writes. Each service is lit for its recording and dark again; CI never holds a key. The browser keeps BYOK keys in `cab.keys.v1` as before and records nothing.
- **D10 — Cassettes in the Workshop. Default: served from the edition as static files and loaded by the Worker through the same `cassetteFor` seam the harness uses.** They are evidence, not content; the budget counts them apart, as it does the Worker.
- **D11 — Generated words. Default: the 35B on the DGX Spark writes every generated utterance, persona line and corpus row, from the labelling guide and the persona's parameters, to a cassette; the 122B and the rule reader label blind; a person reads a 5% sample.** Nothing generated is real (hard rule 9 applies to the generator's output and `checkSynthetic` sweeps it); nothing generated is labelled only by the model that wrote it. The alternative — hand-writing a thousand rows — is a month of Andrew's time, which is what this plan is for.
- **D12 — The story's place in the trace. Default: a render, never a store.** A story is folded from the trace on request, as the assurance pack is; nothing a story shows is anywhere but on the bus. If a story cannot show it, the sensor is missing, and WP159's coverage test is where that is caught.
- **D13 — Replay provenance. Default: on the bundle, never on the trace.** `103-…` §3 chose no trace field because a replay must reproduce the recording's digest; that stands. The bundle carries `replayedFrom` beside its digest (a replayed run's bundle digest therefore differs from the recording's by that field alone, and `verifyBundleDigest` says so), the cell carries the brain as before, and the story reads either. The alternative — a field excluded from the digest's input — changes D21's rule (the digest over parsed events) for one field, and is not taken.
